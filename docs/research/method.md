# Method & evidence

## Classify before comparing

| Category | Working definition | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single-product brand | One core garment/product model, possibly with size or color variants | Current catalog and product navigation |
| T-shirt-focused brand | T-shirts lead the offer, but several styles or other products exist | Catalog plus homepage positioning |
| Broader apparel benchmark | Multiple apparel categories; a white tee is one reference product | Catalog and selected product page |
| Unverified candidate | The business model or comparable product has not been checked | First research pass |

Do not call a brand single-product based only on its origin story or one landing page. If no strict single-product competitors are found, report that limitation and retain the closest focused alternatives.

## Collect one comparable product

Record the exact product name, URL, variant, size, marketed gender, region, currency, and date. Prefer a non-graphic white short-sleeve crew neck. Explain exceptions such as off-white color, undershirt construction, or multipack-only availability.

## Separate evidence from interpretation

- **Observed:** something visible on a dated page or during a documented interaction.
- **Brand claim:** what the seller says; not independently tested.
- **Customer report:** an individual review or interview, with context and sample limitations.
- **Interpretation:** our explanation of what the evidence might mean.
- **Hypothesis:** an idea to test; never present it as a finding.
- **Unknown:** not collected or not disclosed. Unknown does not mean zero or poor performance.

## Capture consistently

For each claim, add a source ID, URL, date accessed, relevant page section, and a short paraphrase. Note promotions and location. Screenshots should have descriptive filenames and omit personal/payment details. Use primary sources for specifications and policies; customer sources for experiences. Do not copy whole reviews or product descriptions.

## Pricing rules

Record list price and current selling price separately. Divide pack price by required shirt count to compare unit prices; keep the minimum checkout amount visible. Keep shipping and tax separate unless a consistent destination and method have been tested. Do not compare a temporary sale against another brand's list price without labeling it.

## Shopping experience check

Check white variant selection, size guide, availability, cart behavior, delivery information, returns, mobile readability, and keyboard navigation. Record the device and date. Stop before placing an order. A quick check is not a full accessibility audit.

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Business category verified against catalog
- [ ] Exact comparable product selected
- [ ] Price, currency, pack size, and date recorded
- [ ] Material, weight if disclosed, fit, sizes, and care documented
- [ ] Shipping and returns sources recorded
- [ ] Positioning and visual choices tied to evidence
- [ ] At least one limitation or unanswered question noted
- [ ] Matrix updated and sources checked
